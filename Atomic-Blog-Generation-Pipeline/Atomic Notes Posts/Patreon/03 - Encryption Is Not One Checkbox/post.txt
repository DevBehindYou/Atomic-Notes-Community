One thing I want Atomic Notes to avoid is vague security language.

Software companies often write: "Your data is encrypted."

That sounds reassuring. It doesn't tell you enough.

Encryption can mean data moving over HTTPS.

It can mean encrypted server disks.

It can mean a database encrypted with a key the provider controls.

Or it can mean content is encrypted on your device before the provider ever receives it.

Those are different threat models.

Atomic Notes offers optional end to end encryption for people who want the stronger model. When the vault is on, a key made on your phone from six words encrypts each note before sync. The vault is off by default, and I say so plainly.

I'd rather explain this distinction again and again than hide behind a simple "encrypted" badge.

Privacy software shouldn't make you guess what its security claims mean.
