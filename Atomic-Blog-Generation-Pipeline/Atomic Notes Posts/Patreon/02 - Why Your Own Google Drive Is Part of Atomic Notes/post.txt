One question shaped a lot of the Atomic Notes architecture:

Where should synced note content actually live?

The easiest answer would have been: "Put everything in my own app database."

That would have made many backend decisions simpler.

It would also have made Atomic Notes the permanent owner of your storage.

I wanted to try something different.

Atomic Notes syncs your notes into a private folder in your own Google Drive. The app can only see the files it created there.

The app stays local first.

The Atomic Notes server coordinates sign-in, sync, versions, sessions, metadata and Atomic Energy. It never stores your note text.

Optional end to end encryption can seal each note on your phone before it syncs.

This isn't the only possible privacy architecture, and it isn't perfect for everyone. Self-hosting, WebDAV, Syncthing and fully offline storage all have their place.

For Atomic Notes, user owned cloud storage felt like a good middle ground between convenience and ownership.

Your notes. Your storage. Your control. ⚛️
