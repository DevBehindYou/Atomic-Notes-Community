A notes app should still be useful when everything around it goes wrong.

Your internet can disappear.

A cloud provider can have an outage.

A backend can go down for a while.

Your phone can bounce between unstable connections.

None of that should stop you from writing down a thought.

That's why Atomic Notes starts on your device.

When you create or edit a note, the local copy isn't an afterthought. It's the foundation of the experience.

Cloud sync comes afterward.

That sounds like a small architectural decision, but it changes how the whole app has to be built. I have to think about:

• unsynced changes
• retries
• conflicting edits
• app restarts
• interrupted uploads
• deletion states
• version tracking
• multiple devices

It's more work than sending every edit straight to a server.

But it supports one of the principles I care about most:

Your ability to use your notes shouldn't depend on permission from the cloud.

That's the kind of software I want Atomic Notes to become.

Local first. Private by design. ⚛️
